Effect of femoral canal shape on mechanical stress distribution and adaptive bone remodelling around a cementless tapered-wedge stem.

单位: Department of Orthopaedic Surgery, Yokohama City University, s3-9, Fukuura, Kanazawa-ku, Yokohama-city, Kanagawa, 236-0004, Japan.
通讯: Y Inaba @ Department of Orthopaedic Surgery, Yokohama City University, s3-9, Fukuura, Kanazawa-ku, Yokohama-city, Kanagawa, 236-00
相关性: 该论文直接研究了非骨水泥锥形柄在不同股骨髓腔形状下的应力分布和骨重塑，与用户的研究方向高度相关。

English summary & terms
Motivation· To investigate how femoral canal shape influences mechanical stress distribution and adaptive bone remodelling around a cementless tapered-wedge stem in total hip arthroplasty.
Method· Subject-specific finite element models of 20 femurs (pre- and post-operative) with varying canal shapes (champagne flute, stovepipe, intermediate) were created to simulate single-leg stance and predict stress distribution.
Result· Stovepipe femurs with large stems exhibited significantly lower equivalent stress in the proximal-medial region, correlating with greater bone mineral density loss at one year, indicating stress shielding.
Validation· Finite element predictions were validated against patient-specific bone mineral density changes measured one year post-operatively.
