English summary & terms
Motivation· To compare the outcomes of conventional versus dual-mobility acetabular components in primary total hip arthroplasty.
Method· A retrospective cohort study of 531 patients (313 conventional, 218 dual-mobility) analyzing major complications within two years.
Result· No significant differences in major complications or dislocation rates were found, but dual-mobility patients had longer hospital stays.
Validation· Clinical retrospective data with statistical analysis.

Forgotten but relevant: The origins of the “safe zone” in total hip arthroplasty

边界: This paper does not quantify stress shielding, micromotion, or implant-bone interface mechanics; it is a historical commentary on acetabular cup orientation, not femoral stem biomechanics or FEA.

English summary & terms
Motivation· To revisit the historical origins and enduring clinical relevance of Lewinnek's safe zone concept for acetabular cup positioning in total hip arthroplasty.
Method· A commentary reviewing the methodology, findings, and limitations of the 1978 Lewinnek study based on 300 primary THAs.
Result· The safe zone (40°±10° inclination, 15°±10° anteversion) remains a benchmark despite measurement errors and evolving surgical technology.
Validation· None; this is a historical commentary without original experimental or simulation data.
