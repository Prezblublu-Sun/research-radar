Motivation· To compare the postoperative effectiveness of a less invasive short external rotator sparing approach versus the standard posterior approach in total hip arthroplasty.
Method· A prospective observational study of 126 patients assigned to either standard or corrective surgical approach, with perioperative and functional outcomes recorded.
Result· The corrective group showed reduced blood loss, shorter hospital stay, faster functional recovery, and better pain scores, while muscle enzyme levels and prosthesis position were similar.
Validation· Clinical validation through postoperative follow-up at 2 and 8 weeks using WOMAC, Harris, Oxford hip scores, and VAS pain assessment.
