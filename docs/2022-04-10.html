Engineering Densely Packed Adipose Tissue via Environmentally Controlled In‐Bath 3D Bioprinting

单位: Department of Mechanical Engineering Pohang University of Science and Technology (POSTECH) Pohang Kyungbuk 37673 Republic of Korea
通讯: Byoung Soo Kim @ School of Biomedical Convergence Engineering Pusan National University Yangsan Kyungnam 50612 Republic of Korea  |  Dong‐Woo Cho @ Department of Mechanical Engineering Pohang University of Science and Technology (POSTECH) Pohang Kyungbuk 37673 Republ
相关性: 该论文未涉及任何AI或机器学习技术，与AI生物打印方向无关。
边界: This paper contains no AI/ML integration into any bioprinting workflow stage; it is purely an experimental method for adipose tissue engineering.
动机· 现有脂肪组织构造物细胞密度低，无法再现天然脂肪形态。
方法· 提出环境控制浴槽3D生物打印，采用混合生物墨水实现高密度细胞增殖。
结果· 成功构建体外致密脂肪组织，并模拟肥胖相关炎症。
验证· 通过4周脂肪生成实验和共培养验证组织成熟与功能。
English summary & terms
Motivation· Current adipose constructs suffer from low cell density, hindering the recreation of native adipose tissue morphology.
Method· Environmentally controlled in-bath 3D bioprinting with a hybrid bioink enables selective proliferation at high cell density.
Result· Densely packed adipose tissue was engineered in vitro, exhibiting obesity-like physiological changes.
Validation· Adipogenesis over 4 weeks and co-culture with monocytes validated tissue maturation and inflammatory response.

Validation· Experimental validation through corrosion and hardness tests.

Motivation· To evaluate whether autologous dentin from periodontally compromised teeth yields comparable outcomes to non-periodontally compromised teeth for lateral ridge augmentation.
Method· A retrospective study comparing complications, resorption, and osseointegration between patient groups receiving dentin grafts from periodontally compromised or healthy teeth.
Result· No statistically significant differences were found in complications or graft resorption between the two groups.
Validation· Clinical follow-up with radiographic assessment of hard tissue loss and implant survival.

相关性: 与研究方向无关，未涉及任何生物力学建模或有限元分析
边界: No biomechanical modeling, no stress/strain analysis, no micromotion, no finite element simulation; purely a clinical templating accuracy study.

English summary & terms
Motivation· To evaluate the accuracy of digital 2D templating in predicting implant sizes for total hip arthroplasty.
Method· A retrospective study of 391 THA patients comparing preoperatively templated cup and stem sizes with actual implanted sizes using mediCAD software.
Result· Templating showed acceptable accuracy with 83.1% of cups and 78.6% of stems within ±2 sizes of the actual implant.
Validation· Comparison of templated sizes to intraoperatively implanted sizes in a clinical cohort.
