英文摘要与术语
Motivation· To determine whether the timing of debridement, antibiotics, and implant retention (DAIR) affects the risk of re-revision within one year after primary total hip or knee arthroplasty.
Method· A retrospective analysis of the Dutch Arthroplasty Register (LROI) was conducted, including all primary THA and TKA that underwent DAIR within 12 weeks postoperatively, with success defined as no re-revision within one year.
Result· No significant difference in one-year re-revision rates was found between DAIR performed within 4 weeks and those performed between 4 and 12 weeks for both hip and knee arthroplasty.
Validation· Clinical registry data with statistical analysis of 207 hip and 209 knee DAIR procedures.

Motivation· To investigate whether surgical scheduling is a modifiable factor affecting postoperative outcomes and costs in lower extremity joint arthroplasty.
Method· A retrospective prognostic study comparing length of stay, costs, and complications between Monday/Tuesday and Thursday/Friday surgeries in 1,571 TKA and 992 THA patients.
Result· Patients undergoing surgery earlier in the week had shorter length of stay and lower costs, even after adjusting for comorbidities.
Validation· Multivariable regression models with odds ratios and confidence intervals.
