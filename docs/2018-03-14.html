Motivation· To investigate the effect of orbital motion mimicking breast movement on fibroblast-silicone interaction, as static in vitro conditions do not reflect the dynamic in vivo environment.
Method· Physicochemical characterization (roughness, wettability, chemistry) of three commercial silicone breast implant shells with different topographies, combined with in vitro dynamic (orbital motion) and static cultures of breast-derived fibroblasts.
Result· Dynamic culture enhanced cell adhesion, proliferation, and expression of focal adhesion kinase (FAK) and alpha-smooth muscle actin (alpha-SMA), indicating mechanosensitivity and contractile force generation.

Validation· Pilot study with small sample size and low T2DM risk, no confirmatory validation.
